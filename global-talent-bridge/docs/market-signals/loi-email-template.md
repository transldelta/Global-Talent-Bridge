# Letter of Intent (LOI) Email Template — CorridorWork

*Market Signal Sprint · 2026-06-09 · Admin use only*

---

## ⚠️ Important

This template is for **buyer or investor LOI requests only** — not for unsolicited outreach.

Only use this template when:
- A contact has already responded with genuine acquisition interest
- They have asked for a formal expression of interest framework
- You have already had at least one real written exchange with them

**Do not send this to cold contacts.**

---

## When to use an LOI

An LOI is not a legal contract — it is a non-binding written expression of intent to negotiate. Purposes:

1. Signals serious buyer intent (elevates from Tier 1 toward Tier 2–3 pricing)
2. Creates documented evidence of buyer interest
3. Provides a structured starting point for due diligence

A **buyer LOI** means the buyer submits the LOI to you.  
A **seller LOI request** means you provide them a template to complete and return.

**4. Due diligence requested**

- [ ] Technical architecture documentation

**5. Escrow**
The buyer agrees that no asset transfer will take place before payment is confirmed in escrow. The buyer accepts the escrow-first transfer process described in the buyer package.

**6. Non-binding**
This letter does not constitute a binding offer or contract. It is an expression of interest only. Either party may withdraw without obligation at any time before a formal signed agreement.

**7. Exclusivity**
This letter does not include exclusivity. The seller may continue other conversations unless a separate exclusivity agreement is signed.

## After receiving a completed LOI

1. Save the signed LOI as a PDF
2. Add an entry to `response-evidence-log-template.md` with evidence link
3. Update `/admin/sale-readiness` sprint notes section
4. Do not move to Tier 2 pricing until at least one LOI is in hand

---

## LOI does NOT guarantee a sale

An LOI:
- Is non-binding
- Does not mean the buyer will proceed
- Does not unlock higher pricing on its own
- Is one signal, not proof of value

Treat it as documentation for future buyers — not as certainty of sale.
